Key facts about the repair process

The work generally starts with a description or review of the damaged area. The repair plan may depend on the condition of nearby sections, the type of fence and whether suitable replacement components are available. Be ready to point out the location and explain when you first noticed the issue, if you know.

Here are general factors to discuss:

  • Coverage: Identify the damaged section, posts, panels, gate or other part you want assessed. Clarify whether nearby areas should also be checked.
  • Timing: The schedule can depend on the extent of the damage, access to the area and the materials or parts needed. Ask for a timing estimate after the work is understood.
  • Cost: The number of affected sections, repair versus replacement needs, material choice, site access and any additional damage can affect the cost. Ask what the estimate includes before approving work.
  • Terms to know: A post supports the fence; a panel is a fence section; a repair scope is the written description of the work being proposed.

For a fair comparison, give each provider the same description of the damage and ask for a clear account of the proposed work. Find out whether the scope includes only the obvious problem or also any connected parts that need attention. A repair proposal is easier to assess when it spells out what will be repaired, what may be replaced and what is not included.
