What fence staining involves

A fence staining contractor McDonough homeowners speak with should first understand the fence itself and the finish you want. Staining is a way to add color and protection to a fence surface, but the right preparation and product depend on the material and its current condition. It is worth discussing the fence's age, any peeling or worn areas, and whether it has been treated before. Those details help set expectations before work is scheduled.

Staining is different from simply choosing a color. The fence may need cleaning and preparation, and the surface may need to be dry before stain goes on. Ask what preparation is included and how the contractor will decide the fence is ready.

Key facts about prep, timing and cost

The scope of staining can include evaluating the fence, preparing its surface, applying stain, and discussing care after the work. The exact steps depend on the fence and should be confirmed with the contractor. Timing can vary with the amount of preparation, the size of the area, access to the fence, and conditions needed for the surface and product. Ask for the expected schedule for your specific job rather than relying on a general time estimate.

Cost also depends on the project, not just the color selected. The fence's length and condition, preparation needs, material, access, and any repairs that need attention can all affect the work involved. Ask for a written description of what is included so you can compare proposals fairly.

Useful terms to know:
- Preparation: Cleaning or other steps taken before stain is applied.
- Coverage: The area a stated amount of stain is expected to coat.
- Coat: One application layer of stain. Ask how many are planned and why.
